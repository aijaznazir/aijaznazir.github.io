Linear algebra · A six-chapter series

Linear algebra, as geometry

Most courses begin with rules for manipulating arrays of numbers. This series begins with pictures: arrows, grids, and the motions of the plane that matrices describe. Each rule then becomes something you can see, and drag.

EACH FRAME: THE GRID MOVED BY ONE 2×2 MATRIX

The one idea underneath

A 2×2 matrix is a complete description of a motion of the plane that keeps the origin fixed and keeps grid lines straight, parallel and evenly spaced. Its two columns say where the arrows \(\hat\imath = (1,0)\) and \(\hat\jmath = (0,1)\) end up, and that is enough to know where every other point goes. Every chapter below is that idea seen from another side: multiplication is doing two motions in a row, the determinant is how the motion scales area, inverses undo it, and eigenvectors are the directions it leaves pointing the same way.

Everything happens in two dimensions, where every step can be drawn. The definitions and arguments are chosen so that they carry over to higher dimensions unchanged.
